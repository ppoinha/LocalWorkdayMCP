Security and privacy

Your assistant gets your access, not more

How access works, what stays on your computer and what to check before you roll it out.

Two layers on every request

1

The API Client sets the outer limit

When your administrator registers the client, they choose its scopes. The server can only reach resources inside them, whoever signs in.

2

You set what comes back

You sign in with your own Workday account and approve the client. Workday then applies your security permissions to every call.

Read-only, within your Workday permissions

  • DaySight MCP only reads from Workday. It has no tools that change data.
  • Answers show what the signed-in person may already see in Workday. Workday security, the API Client scopes and that person's own permissions decide what comes back.
  • Worker names, work email and work phone are returned when you ask for them, as is other data your Workday permissions allow. Names and contact details of job candidates are never returned.
  • Personal contact details, national IDs and bank details are always removed. Religion and sexual orientation are never read.

What stays on your computer

  • The client secret is kept in Claude Desktop's secure storage.
  • Your login token is stored encrypted in your Windows user profile.
  • Secrets and tokens never go into tool arguments, results or logs, so the model never sees them.
  • There is no generic "call any URL" tool. Your assistant can only use the Workday operations the server exposes.
  • Audit logs record the tool name, argument names, status and duration, never values.

Claude Enterprise or ChatGPT Enterprise for privacy and safety

Whatever your assistant retrieves is sent to the AI provider as part of the conversation. For HR data, use Claude Enterprise or ChatGPT Enterprise with data sharing and model training turned off. Both providers say that business data is not used for training by default, so check these settings in your own plan, and check your company's policy on sending HR data to an AI service.

A checklist before you start

  • Have your HRIS administrator register the API Client, and never share a client secret.
  • Give it the smallest set of scopes that covers your questions.
  • Start on a sandbox or implementation tenant.
  • Use Claude Enterprise or ChatGPT Enterprise rather than a consumer account, with data sharing and model training turned off.
